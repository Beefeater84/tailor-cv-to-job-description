# CV: projects

Projects outside the candidate's jobs (own, open source, study, a small project made for the vacancy) that show a skill the vacancy asks for.

### PROJ-01 — Projects that close a requirement the jobs do not show
- Applies to: CV / projects
- Rule: a projects section after the experience (GEN-06) holds the projects from the profile that answer requirements of the vacancy or the group, first of all requirements the jobs do not show. One entry: the name, what it is, which requirement it shows, the result, and a link when there is one. With no relevant project, there is no section.
- Why: hiring managers take work examples as proof of a skill. A project closes a gap that the experience leaves open, and a listed skill with no proof behind it counts for little.
- Sources: N1Fm4JYae1I-en.md (section "Rule 5: Demonstrate Your AI Skills", 10:36): with no workplace example, do a small project related to the job and list it in a separate section; extended from AI skills to any requirement: owner
- Agreement: 1 of 4 authors, no conflicts
- Weight: 0.5
- Needs: profile block with projects; requirements of the vacancy or the group; the experience, to find what it does not show
- Check: every project answers a requirement of the vacancy or the group; every project is in the profile
- Limits: only projects from the profile. A project the agent suggested (GEN-03) goes in only after the candidate has done it and it is added to the profile.

## Open questions

- **Projects before experience.** For a career change, a project may be the main proof of the new skills, and after the experience it may be missed. To decide: whether the section moves above the experience in that case, and how the agent tells that case.
