# CV: competencies and skills

Two lists under the summary: competencies (areas of work, such as "technical sourcing" or "stakeholder management") and skills (software, tools, specific methodologies, languages). Both are built from the requirements of the vacancy, as GEN-08 sets for the whole CV.

### SKILL-01 — Skills: from the vacancy's requirements to the profile
- Applies to: CV / skills
- Rule: a skills section after the competencies and before the experience (GEN-06). It is built from the vacancy, not from the profile:
  1. Take the skills the vacancy asks for. In the job family mode, take the skills of all vacancies in the group; a skill asked by more vacancies goes higher.
  2. Look for each one in the profile. Found: it goes into the CV, worded as the vacancy words it. Not found: the agent asks the candidate (GEN-03); confirmed similar experience is added, otherwise it is reported as a gap.
  3. Skills in the profile that the vacancy does not ask for stay out of the CV; they stay in the profile.
  There is no cap on skills the vacancy asks for, and no skills it does not ask for.
- Why: recruiters and ATS search CVs by the vacancy's keywords. Skills taken from the vacancy and worded like it are found; skills the vacancy does not need take space and blur the fit.
- Sources: 0T8q3B82PRw-en.md (section "Highlighting Skills and Experience", 05:55); 4Qh7C5AA34o-en.md (sections "Adding Skills and Projects", 09:19, and "Customizing the Resume", 10:41); position before experience: owner; group order and vacancy wording: our addition
- Agreement: 2 of 4 authors. Conflicts: count — 0T8q3B82PRw-en.md says ten at most (written for the human reader), 4Qh7C5AA34o-en.md says as many relevant skills as possible (written for the ATS); see GEN-09 and the open question "Number of skills". Position — 4Qh7C5AA34o-en.md puts skills last; owner chose before experience, to be tried.
- Weight: 0.8
- Needs: CV block with skills; skills used in the experience; requirements of the vacancy or of each vacancy in the group
- Check: every skill in the CV is in the vacancy's (or group's) requirements and in the profile, or was confirmed by the candidate; every asked skill is either in the CV, asked about, or reported as a gap; the section comes after the competencies and before the experience
- Limits: only real skills that the candidate would talk about in an interview. No buzzwords. Every skill passes the tests of GEN-12: a must-have skill also has a bullet or a project that uses it, and the list does not follow the vacancy's order.

### SKILL-02 — Competencies: areas of work the vacancy needs
- Applies to: CV / competencies
- Rule: a short list of competencies right under the summary text, built the same way as the skills (SKILL-01): from the areas of work the vacancy (or the group) asks for, matched to the profile.
- Why: the hiring manager checks the fit right after the summary. A list of the areas the vacancy needs, which the candidate has, shows the fit at a glance.
- Sources: 4Qh7C5AA34o-en.md (sections "Work Experience and Education", 04:36, and "Adding Skills and Projects", 09:19: competencies are areas, skills are software and methodologies)
- Agreement: 1 of 4 authors, no conflicts
- Weight: 0.6
- Needs: areas of work from the candidate's experience; requirements and responsibilities of the vacancy or the group
- Check: every competency is asked for by the vacancy (or the group) and backed by the profile
- Limits: only areas the candidate has worked in; a competency is not a skill or a tool

### SKILL-03 — A language only when the vacancy asks for it
- Applies to: CV / skills
- Rule: when the vacancy names a language (for example, English for a vacancy written in another language), the skills section gives that language with the candidate's level, worded as the vacancy words it (for example, "English: C1"). A language the vacancy does not name is not added.
- Why: the level of a required language is checked as yes or no, by the recruiter, the AI screener or a screening question with an automatic rejection. A CV that does not state it reads as "no". A language the vacancy does not need takes space like any other skill it does not ask for (SKILL-01).
- Sources: 6N6H_KQxPWE-ru.md (section "Проблемы с ИИ в рекрутинге", 29:54): "Do you know English at such a level?" as a screening question with an automatic rejection; only when the vacancy names it, in the skills: owner
- Agreement: 1 of 4 authors, no conflicts
- Weight: 0.7
- Needs: languages with their levels from the profile; the languages the vacancy or the group asks for
- Check: every language the vacancy names is in the skills with a level, when the profile has it; no language the vacancy does not name
- Limits: only the level from the profile. A required language the candidate does not have, or has at a lower level, is reported to the candidate as a gap (GEN-03), never raised.

## Open questions

- **Number of skills.** The right answer is not known: ten at most suits the human reader, as many as possible suits the ATS (GEN-09). Tried for now: no cap on skills the vacancy asks for, no skills it does not ask for, so the vacancy itself limits the list. To check on real results; the fallback is a cap of ten in the section, with the other keywords placed in the experience bullets. N1Fm4JYae1I-en.md (section "Rule 2: Tailor Your Resume Effectively", 03:47) found that CVs with the most keywords got fewer interviews, but it puts the cause in keywords without backing, not in their count; this supports the current choice (only skills the vacancy asks for and the profile backs, GEN-12). What to measure on real results: the share of skills with no bullet behind them, not only their number.
